**2026-08-31 (v2.48.3): the rates were never the whole story — snapshots now record the app version.** Nicky, on an accepted job whose drift card had just told him nothing on Rates or Settings had changed: *"This still doesn't make sense, nothing changed."* He was right, twice over: nothing on the job had changed either.

The card's line-level breakdown (v2.47.1) is what made it findable. One room moved — *"Entrance Hall and Landing (including staircase and airing cupboard)" +£24.55* — and every other line drifted by pennies, which is just the proportional spread following the total. One room, full of doors and frames, and no rate touched. **`01564b5`, 27 August, the same day the quote was accepted: doors and frames stopped carrying their own `doorFramePrepPct` and started carrying the ROOM's prep uplift.** One prep decision per room instead of two was the right call for a form filled in on site, and it re-prices every already-accepted quote holding a room whose prep sits above the old door/frame default. Confirmed by running both builds side by side and pricing one identical room object through each: **£567.86 at v2.39.6, £618.21 at v2.48.2** — same settings, same inputs, 8.87% apart, all of it in `doorCost`/`frameCost`. A hall with the airing cupboard and every bedroom door off the landing is exactly where that lands hardest, and it is the only room in the job where it shows.

So the snapshot was recording half of what prices a job. `rates` holds the ~90 numbers; **the code that turns a room into a figure is the other half, and it ships.** Every check the card made passed while the figure moved anyway, and its conclusion — *"the difference is in the job itself"* — sent someone to look at rooms nobody had touched. `appVersion` is now captured into every snapshot (from the baked build literal synchronously, so a capture made on site with no signal still records one, then corrected by `/api/version`), and the card reads three ways: **same build** → the difference really is the job's scope; **different build** → it names them (*"v2.45.2 when this was accepted, v2.48.2 now — a release that changes how a room is costed moves the figure with nothing on the job touched"*); **no version** (every snapshot frozen before this, including the one that raised the question) → it says it cannot tell which, rather than blaming the job. Admitting the app might be the author beats sending its owner hunting through rooms he never edited. Nothing about the agreed figures moves: this is all read-side, the snapshot stays append-only, and Amend is still the only thing that re-prices anything. Verified against a real Postgres across all three branches, plus test:snapshots 40/40, test:scope 188, test:floors 46, test:reorder 37, test:calibration and check:snapshots green.

**2026-08-31 (v2.48.1): the drift card names the room, and stops calling a backup export a rate change.** Shipped v2.47.0's card on a real accepted job and it got two things wrong on the same screen. It compared the **whole** settings blob, and that blob carries `lastBackupExportAt`, `blockedDays`, `icsKey`, `paymentType`/`paymentWeeks`, the branding and the text templates alongside the rates. **Exporting a backup was enough to trip it.** The comparison is now scoped to the keys that can actually move a price — the `SETTINGS_FIELDS` scalars plus `coverageRates`, `kitchenRates`, `materials` and `standaloneRounding` — and a key the snapshot *predates* is not counted as a change either, since a field that did not exist when the quote was frozen cannot have moved its figures. When nothing priced has moved it now says so plainly: *"Nothing that prices this job has changed on the Rates or Settings pages — every rate, the coverage table, the kitchen rates and the product choices are exactly as they were when this was accepted."* A wrong cause stated confidently is the thing this card exists to stop, and the first version was still doing it.

**And it stopped one question short.** *"Labour £3,336.03 → £3,361.42"* is better than blaming the rates and still leaves you opening rooms one at a time. A **Which lines moved** section now sits under the totals: `buildAcceptedQuoteSnapshot()` prices the job as it stands in exactly the shape the frozen record is stored in (it is what Amend previews from), so the two line lists compare directly — room by room, **sorted biggest mover first**, with lines **added since** acceptance and lines **no longer in the job** called out as such. **2026-08-31 (v2.47.0): a declined variation was inside the quote total, and the drift aside blamed the rates for it.** Two bugs found by pulling on one question — *why is this accepted job different at today's rates when the rates haven't changed?*

**The money one.** Summary built the original-scope total as `tcOrig = tcS - varLabour`, and `varLabour` deliberately **excludes declined** variations (they were offered and turned down, so they are worth nothing in the variations subtotal). Net effect: a declined variation's labour was subtracted from nothing and stayed **inside the quote-facing total** — the hero figure, the deposit and the payment plan — while the variations line beside it read *"+ £0.00 variations"*. Reproduced against a real database: a £1,030 job with one room flagged Variation/Declined showed £1,615. Approved and pending were always right; declined was the one status that leaked. The client quote, the Xero quote, the final invoice and the accepted-quote snapshot all filter on `isVariation` **alone**, so Summary was the single reader disagreeing with the document actually being sent — on a draft it inflated the quote you were about to send, and on an accepted job it surfaced as phantom drift with nothing having changed. `computeVariationsView()` now keeps **two sets of sums for two different questions**: `varLabour`/`varTime`/`varSpray` (non-declined — what the variations are worth) and `varLabourAll`/`varTimeAll`/`varSprayAll` (every flagged item — what is not part of the original quote), and Summary subtracts the second. The variations subtotal, the "+ £X variations" line and the profitability view are untouched.

**The one that made it invisible.** The aside under the hero read *"Same job at today's rates: £X — not what was agreed"*, which names a **cause** rather than stating a fact — and usually the wrong cause, since every input feeds that figure and the rates are the thing most likely NOT to have moved. It now reads *"Same job priced today: £X — £Y more, broken down below"*, and a new **"Where the £Y difference is"** card attributes it from the snapshot alone: **Labour** and **Materials** agreed → today, each marked moved or unchanged, with the **adjustment pool** (standalone upcharge + the round-up to the next £5) pulled into its own row so that an extra tin of paint — which changes where the total lands against the next £5 — stops reading as labour having moved. Then the sentence that answers the question: the snapshot stores the whole settings blob verbatim, so the scalars are diffed **by name** using the Rates page's own labels (read off the inputs that page already renders — a second table of human names would be one more thing to keep in step), and the entire blob is compared besides, so *"**No rate or setting has changed since acceptance** — the difference is in the job itself"* is only ever said when it is true. Rates that did move are named: *"Day Rate 300 → 325"*. A snapshot rebuilt from a Xero total carries no rates and says so rather than guessing. The card renders only when the two figures differ, and reading it writes nothing. Verified in a real browser against a real Postgres: the declined-variation leak before and after (and approved/pending still routing to the variations line), the card on a materials-only change reporting labour unchanged and no rate change, the same job with a day-rate change naming it, `test:snapshots` 40/40, `check:snapshots`, `test:scope` and `test:floors` green.

**2026-08-31 (v2.45.5): the login gate becomes once per phone, so it can actually be switched on.** Nicky, on the client approval link: *"it's the ability to trim the url and access my app that bothers me — I currently like not worrying about typing a password every time I open the app."* Both halves of that are right, and the second was based on a misreading the code invited. `APP_PASSWORD` (`MULTI_INSTANCE_PILOT_SPEC.md` WS1) was never a per-opening password: it has always been a Postgres-backed session with a 30-day cookie, so once a month per device. But `rolling` was not set, which means the cookie counted down from the LOGIN rather than from last use — a phone opened every single day still got thrown back to the password screen on day thirty. That reads as "this app asks for a password", and it is precisely the friction that keeps the gate unset. **`rolling: true` fixes it in one line:** the cookie is re-sent on every response, so its expiry moves forward with use and a phone that opens the app at least monthly logs in exactly once, ever. Verified rather than assumed — the stored cookie's expiry advanced by exactly the elapsed time across two requests. **Safe with `saveUninitialized:false`**, and that was verified too: a client reading `/q/<token>` has no session, so nothing is created and **zero** `Set-Cookie` headers reach them; only a signed-in device carries one and only that device's expiry rolls. **Why this matters now rather than at WS1:** v2.41.0 put a client-facing link on the app's own origin, and *every* unmatched path there serves the SPA — so a client who trims the link back to the domain arrives at the app's front door. The URL's shape was never going to be what stops that (a shortener would be worse: it hands the token to a third party and puts a guessable alias in front of a 192-bit one), and a custom domain changes only what the door is called. The gate is the control, which makes it load-bearing where it used to be optional. Also fixed on the way past: **`public/login.html` carried `autocomplete="off"` on the form**, which suppresses the offer to save the password in iCloud Keychain — turning the rare re-login back into typing, on the one screen where autofill is the whole point. ## Architecture reminder

- **Frontend:** `public/index.html` — single-file vanilla JS app
- **Backend:** `routes/xero.js` (OAuth + quote/contact/item endpoints), `routes/api.js` (rooms, exterior items, settings, HSL)
- **Database:** PostgreSQL on Render (rooms, exterior_items, settings, hsl_state tables)
- **Deploy:** push to GitHub `main` → Render auto-deploys
- **Xero:** connects with `accounting.contacts`, `accounting.settings.read`, `accounting.invoices`, `accounting.banktransactions` and `accounting.payments` scopes (the last two added 2026-08-19 for deposits, `DEPOSITS_SPEC.md` — proved permitted first via `GET /auth/scope-check`). **This app uses GRANULAR scopes** (see its developer-portal Authorisation list): `accounting.invoices` is the valid quotes+invoices scope here, and the coarse `accounting.transactions` is NOT permitted — requesting it fails the whole auth with `invalid_scope` (the 2026-07-22/23 reconnect saga, resolved v1.10.6). Old doubts about `accounting.invoices` being "not a documented scope" were wrong for this app type.

## Key gotchas learned during the build

- **Duplicate functions:** the app has had repeated bugs where an old version of a function survived alongside a new one, with the later definition silently overriding the earlier. When something doesn't behave as expected, grep for duplicate `function X` definitions first.
- **extItems vs old extCost:** exterior items use the `extItems` array + `calcExtItem()`, loaded from `/api/extitems`. Old code used `extCost`/`extTime` globals — make sure any new code (Xero quote, CSV) reads from `extItems`, not the old globals.
- **Data sync:** rooms and exterior items load from the server into memory on init. Render functions should read from the in-memory arrays. Avoid reintroducing localStorage as a competing source of truth.
- **Whole-tin rounding must happen at job level**, not per room, or multi-room jobs over-count tins.

## FEATURE: Realistic time estimate (scheduling) ✅ SHIPPED

**As built:** Settings carry `overheadMins` (default 45 mins/day) and `bufferPct` (default 0), which feed an `onSiteDays` figure shown alongside working time. `onSiteDays` is what `computeDepositPlan()` derives the weekly instalment count from — the key link below, wired as specced. No costing change: the quote total is untouched.

The app calculates **working time** (hands-on hours ÷ day length) — accurate for costing, but not calendar reality. A job at 2.85 working days actually spans more calendar days once drying, floor protection, setup, masking and clearing up are counted. Those non-productive elements are currently only in the markup (cost), not reflected in the *time*.

**This is NOT a costing change** — the quote total stays exactly as-is. It's a parallel "realistic duration" figure for scheduling, shown alongside working time:
- **Working time** — e.g. 2.85 days (drives labour/cost, unchanged)
- **Estimated on site** — e.g. ~4 days (what to block out in the diary)

### Approach — per-day overhead + optional buffer
- **Per-day overhead** (Settings, editable) — a fixed non-productive allowance per day on site (setup, protection, masking, cleanup — say 45–60 mins). Reduces effective productive hours/day, which stretches calendar days. Scales correctly: more days = more accumulated setup/cleanup.
- **Optional buffer multiplier** (Settings) — a modest × for drying/slippage on top, if wanted.
- Keep it to "realistic days on site" — don't try to model drying times precisely or manage the diary; that's calendar territory.

### Why it must come before the deposit feature (KEY LINK)
The staged-payment logic keys off job length — and it must use REALISTIC time, not working time. Example: 6.85 working days looks like just over one week, but realistically spans **two working weeks**, which is exactly when weekly staged payments apply. If the payment logic used raw working days it could treat a two-week job as single-week and miss the staging.

Chain for staged payments: working time → realistic time (× overhead/buffer) → working weeks (realistic days ÷ 5, or however weeks fall) → number of weekly payments.

### Client-facing (optional)
Having the realistic duration to hand is also better for telling the client "about a week" — the raw working time would sound too short and set wrong expectations.

The Colours tab lets each colour number carry a name (e.g. colour 1 = "Dimity"). Colour names/codes now autofill as you type, so exact names and codes are to hand for ordering from the merchant.

### What shipped
- **Data model** — each colour stored as `{ name, brand, code }` in a `colour_library` table. **Global and permanent**: NOT job-scoped, and deliberately untouched by Clear Rooms / Clear Everything (it's a reference list, not job data).
- **Seeded with Farrow & Ball + Little Greene** — 509 colours (301 F&B, 208 Little Greene) from `db/colour-library-seed.json`, loaded once via `db/seed-colour-library.js`. Notably larger than the ~130-each the plan assumed. (Since grown to 1221 entries — Dulux, Dulux Heritage and Paint & Paper Library were added the same way, and RAL Classic added 2026-07-30: all 213 standard RAL Classic colours. RAL is the one brand stored name-first-as-code — `name` is the RAL number, e.g. `"RAL 7016"`, and `code` holds the descriptive name, e.g. `"Anthracite grey"` — since the number, not the name, is what's actually used to specify/order a RAL colour.)
- **Autocomplete on the Colours tab** — typing 2+ characters filters the library and shows up to 8 matches (name, brand, code); picking one fills in brand + code. Mirrors the existing Xero contact autocomplete pattern (`onXeroClientInput`), but filters in memory rather than hitting a search endpoint — ~500 entries are cheap to filter on every keystroke.
- **Grows on first use** — an unknown colour offers "+ Save X to your colour library", which takes brand + code and POSTs to `/api/colour-library` (upsert on name+brand). The personal list grows over time; no cross-brand database needed.
- **Free-text fallback** — "Skip" still commits the typed name as a plain label with no brand/code, so an unknown colour never blocks the flow.

  - The save option is **always** offered, even when the name already exists — it just reads "+ Add X under another brand" instead of "+ Save X to your colour library". Previously an existing name suppressed the option entirely, making a second brand's version of that name unaddable.

- **API** — `GET/POST /api/colour-library` in `routes/api.js`.

- **Built earlier than planned.** The roadmap had this as low priority behind materials + deposit; it shipped ahead of the deposit feature.
- **localStorage IS used here, deliberately** — `pe-colour-library` caches the library client-side for instant autocomplete offline/on site. This is a READ CACHE of a global reference list, refreshed from the server on init — not a competing source of truth for job data. The "no localStorage" rule in the architecture notes still applies to rooms/exterior/colours.

- Colour label stays reference/ordering only; the colour NUMBER still drives the materials calculation (see MATERIALS_SPEC.md).
- Big trade brands (Dulux etc.) are usually colour-matched anyway — the grow-on-use path handles them, no wholesale seeding.

Headlines from the spec:
- **Materials are quoted as an ESTIMATE and invoiced as USED.** This is the billing model, and it makes tracking load-bearing rather than a safety net: actuals ARE the invoice's materials list, not just a note of what got forgotten. Labour is quoted and billed as quoted; only materials float.
- **Quantities only in, money derived out — all of it.** Account codes confirmed: **202** sales, **311** paint cost, **314** specific-sundry-item cost. All ride on the `/Items` payload the app already fetches, so billable value AND margin come from a typed quantity. No manual pricing anywhere.
- **314 is NOT the cost side of the sundries %.** The % (labour × %) covers stock consumables used across jobs — caulk, tape, filler, floor protection — at normal usage, and stays a percentage; the % itself is never itemised, never tracked. 314 is for job-specific consumables the % won't cover (wallpaper paste, lining paper), which are real Xero items tracked like paint. ## Xero quote PDF templates (custom DOCX)

The Xero quote branding uses **custom DOCX templates** (downloaded from Xero → Settings → branding themes). These are editable via Claude Code (docx skill), unlike Xero's built-in theme editor. Use this to make the growing quote — materials line items, sundries line, deposit/payment terms — display cleanly, especially a long itemised materials list that looks cramped in the default layout.

**How they work:** Word documents with Xero **merge fields** (e.g. `«LineDescription»`, `«LineAmount»`, `«Total»`) that Xero swaps for real data at generation time. Claude Code can restyle/rearrange layout, spacing, columns, fonts and the arrangement of merge fields.

**Constraints / cautions:**
- Can only use merge fields Xero actually supports, named EXACTLY — can't invent new ones. If you want data shown (e.g. deposit amount), there must be a Xero merge field for it, or it comes through as a line item / terms text.
- A branding theme can have separate templates (quote, invoice, etc.) — be explicit which one is being edited. The quote has multiple pages (cover letter, itemised quote, terms, cancellation) — specify which.
- **BACK UP the working template before editing.** A renamed field or broken structure can make quotes generate wrong or fail to populate. Edit a copy, keep the original safe.
- **Test on a real draft quote in Xero before going live** — merge-field templates can look right in Word but only prove out when Xero renders them with real data. Check branding, materials flow, totals and terms all populate.

**Process:** download current template from Xero → give the DOCX to Claude Code → tell it what's being added and how it should look → it edits keeping branding + merge fields intact → re-upload to Xero → test on a draft.
